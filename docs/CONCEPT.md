# COMB / 001 — Concept

## Original dream material

In a dream, a production company periodically released stream of consciousness films. One installment concerned human memory: music, people, scenes, and fragments that were difficult to explain. An accompanying app was called **COMB / 梳子**. Its surviving description was **“反映着人类记忆中粘连的部分。”** (“reflecting the parts of human memory that stick together”). The idea felt intelligible in the dream, but could not be fully expressed after waking.

This is the boundary of the original dream account. The interaction, five principles, copy, and visual system below are artistic interpretations made later. They are not presented as additional facts from the dream.

## Artistic interpretation

COMB is an experimental interactive artwork about memory's entangled parts. In subjective experience, a person, sound, place, and feeling can seep into one another until the memories cannot be fully restored as separate events. A song can belong to several summers; an event can vanish while its feeling remains.

**Entanglement** means this shared atmosphere or perceptual texture, rather than a mere link between two independent items. It is an artistic term without a claimed scientific correspondence to quantum entanglement. COMB does not read, diagnose, identify, or objectively reconstruct real memories.

A comb ordinarily separates tangled hair. Here the gesture is reversed: following the grain of memory reveals its entanglement. **To comb is not necessarily to separate. / 梳理，并不一定意味着分离。 / 梳かすことは、必ずしも切り離すことではない。**

The visitor encounters image-like composition, text, optional synthesized sound, and interaction. Two composed fragments can meet in many combinations; an echo and a persistent thread make that encounter perceptible. No single interpretation is scored as correct. A private note lets the visitor keep what remains.

## Five principles

1. **Association / 联想 / 連想** — Nearness in memory differs from nearness in time. Spatial proximity and selection bring fragments together.
2. **Entanglement / 粘连 / 絡み合い** — A connection becomes shared texture. Fragments gain a common line and visual warmth while retaining their words.
3. **Residue / 残留 / 残り香** — A feeling can remain after details fade. The connection persists when focus moves away.
4. **Coexistence / 共存 / 共存** — Different versions of a past may remain together. Multiple relationships coexist without replacing the original fragment.
5. **Shared Memory / 共同记忆 / 共有する記憶** — People can form different associations with the same work; an experience together becomes another memory. The field is open to individual readings, without publishing anyone's private text.

The fifth principle is conceptual rather than a multi-user feature in this first version. No public community or shared database is implied.
